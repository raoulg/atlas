Every night `next` is measured on one GitHub runner type: the clean release build, `atlas view` of Atlas
and of the programs `laws.yml` reads, cold and warm, with how long reading, checking, `flow::of` and the
page take, and every test binary. The numbers are kept as a series on the branch `perf-data`, drawn as
charts in its README. A measure that lies past the median of its last ten runs on two nights in a row is
reported in the run's summary and in one tracking issue (label `performance`). No commit or landing runs
anything for it (`docs/reference/performance.md`).

The chart shows a test of it on a laptop, ten runs on a small subject: six of the build as it is, three
with a wrapper that sleeps 1.5 s before `atlas`, then one without again. The first slow run is suspect,
the second is reported and opens the issue, the third stays marked slower without a second comment:

```text
### Performance of `0888888`

**Slower**: 2 measure(s) lie past their baseline on this run and the run before.

| measure                      | this run | baseline | change  | past by more than |        |
| ---------------------------- | -------: | -------: | ------: | ----------------: | ------ |
| `read/pipeline/cold/seconds` |   1.55 s |   0.05 s | +2843%  |            0.50 s | slower |
| `read/pipeline/warm/seconds` |   1.52 s |   0.01 s | +11967% |            0.50 s | slower |
```

The phases `--timings` reports stayed within their baselines: the sleep is outside Atlas.
