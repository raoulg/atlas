To know what a Python body's own code uses, Atlas asks of every name where it is bound, what it stands for and
whether it belongs to the body, and each of those questions asks tree-sitter for the parents of the nodes around the
name. tree-sitter finds a node's parent by going down from the root of its file, so each answer cost the file up to
that node, and the names 0.71 follows through names computed of names and through changes in place asked it more
often: the `body` stage grew until a cold reading of a Python program took a fifth to a third longer than at 0.70.0.
A body is now read as the Rust reader reads one: its parents and its walks for one kind of node after another are
answered from one walk of it, kept while the body is read. Nothing Atlas reads, draws or checks changes:
`atlas-code --no-cache analyze` gives the same bytes before and after on 105 subjects (goad_toolkit and vectormesh at
the revisions of `tools/corpus.txt`, Atlas at 0.70.0, and both sides of every Python pair under `engine/tests/pairs`).

Bars: goad_toolkit cold, 0.70.0 = 0.425 s; goad_toolkit cold, before = 0.601 s; goad_toolkit cold, after = 0.295 s; vectormesh cold, 0.70.0 = 0.576 s; vectormesh cold, before = 0.766 s; vectormesh cold, after = 0.384 s | `atlas-code --no-cache view --no-open --timings` of each program at `--libraries 1`, an empty cache and no index, release builds of 0.70.0 (`4ebbbae0`), `next` at `d90c452f` and `py-410` at `acd6b3b9`, seven interleaved rounds on a developer machine (Apple silicon, other work running), the median of each; every round after was faster than every round of 0.70.0. The `body` stage went from 0.405 s to 0.117 s on goad_toolkit and from 0.510 s to 0.136 s on vectormesh.
