`ruimtelijke-knowledge-graph`, a Python program with a TypeScript app beside it that reads `@tanstack` and `@vitest`, reads
cold in 16 s instead of 89 s: its 39 `@tanstack` bodies in 0.1 s instead of 57 s, its 159 `@vitest` bodies in 0.6 s instead of
14 s. A long function, of thousands of calls of one name, reads in seconds instead of minutes. The picture is the same:
`atlas-code analyze --no-cache` writes the same bytes for that program, for Atlas itself and for each of the 460 programs
under `engine/tests` (the pairs, the plans and the fixtures, in all three languages).

What a TypeScript name holds where it stands is a function of its binding, the depth it is followed at and the names the
body has kept so far. The reader followed every occurrence anew, so a name that stands for another twice along eight
bindings was unfolded along 2^8 occurrences, at every call that hands it. Each name's values are now found once and shared
by its occurrences and its calls, each value once. A body's own questions (where a name is read, whether a step stands in
the arm of a guard, whether a function written in place binds a name) looked again at every statement of the block a call
stands in, for every call; what they look for is now found once per body or block.

Bars: Reading rkg, cold, before = 89 s; Reading rkg, cold, after = 16 s; @tanstack bodies, before = 57 s; @tanstack bodies, after = 0.1 s; 5000 calls of one name, before = 297 s; 5000 calls of one name, after = 4 s | `atlas-code view --no-cache --no-open --timings` of `ruimtelijke-knowledge-graph` at `a663ff9` (the `read` phase, and the `body` stage of `@tanstack`), and `atlas-code analyze --no-cache` of a generated TypeScript function of 5000 calls `show(first)`, on a laptop (Apple M-series) shared with other builds, one run each, before and after one after the other.
