A loop over a tuple, list or set written in place is named by what it binds and how many members it holds, `for layer
in 4 layers`, and no longer by the whole literal's source; its members are the steps. In a comprehension whose later loop
goes over such a literal (`[layer for a, b in pairwise(widths) for layer in (Linear(a, b), Dropout(p))]`), the loop
before it is the loop, `for a, b in pairwise(widths)`, and the members are what one turn of it does. Rust's `for x in
[a, b]` and TypeScript's `for (const x of [a, b])` are named the same way.

A call through a table of the module's own looked up by a value, `ACTIVATIONS[config.activation]()` with `ACTIVATIONS =
{"gelu": nn.GELU, ..}`, is a choice between the table's entries, as a `match` on the value with a case per key is: the
page draws a frame `one of · config.activation` with an arm per entry, each under its key, and the route reads `GELU()
· when config.activation is "gelu"`. It was one step that gave four values of no name.
