A route that gives back a number, a string, a list or an option now ends at that value: a box on the right labelled by
the type its signature gives (`float`, `f64`, `Option<String>`, `list[bytes]`), as a route that gives back a type of the
program ends at that type. Before, such a value was drawn as a wire from its maker to its taker, and at the route's
border the taker is the caller, beyond the picture, so the route ended at no place: `loudest -> float` drew a box that
gave nothing. What a method of a class route gives back ends there the same way. Inside a route nothing changes: a
result of no type of the program's still goes back to the step that called it.

A class with no methods, built from its fields (a dataclass, a struct, an interface), is a route whose door is its
constructor: `Level` ends at the `Level` it builds, as a class with an initializer does. An initializer drawn as a route
of its own builds its class.

A route whose code names nothing it gives back, changes, writes or raises ends at no place either, and that is the
program's to answer: it is dead, or does what Atlas does not see. The law `every_route_ends_at_a_place` now runs, and
says these routes as findings of the program rather than faults of the picture; where the code does give something and
the picture still draws nothing, the route is marked with its issue: a test that only asserts (#446), a TypeScript
`POST` to its own server (#447), a handler that changes a value its module keeps (#448).

Kind: drawn wrong, now right.
