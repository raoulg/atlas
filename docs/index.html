The same program. The same figure.

Fetch sunspot counts from the web, keep the CSV, clean it, smooth it, draw a figure. Written twice in Python: once as a sloppy script, once typed and elegant. Atlas draws what each one does.

the monthly mean sunspot number since 1749 (SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium), smoothed over 13 months · drawn by the typed pipeline

Both reach the same places: the web, the raw CSV, the figure. Inside they part. In the sloppy script every step reads and changes one global, Global(sunspots.df); nothing is handed from step to step. In the typed one a DataFrame goes from load to smooth to Trend.save, and the command names every file. A red dot is a question Atlas asks the author: why do get_data and plot pick their own files, and what do the four functions give back?

Ported to Rust. A picture alike.

Code gets rewritten in another language: for speed, for one binary to ship, for a team, and more and more often by an agent. Tests tell you the port gives the same figure. They don't tell you whether the design survived: the same steps, the same values handed between them, the same places. Atlas draws every language in the same terms, so the original and the port can lie side by side: the same shape where the design survived, and differences where it did not, or where the language works otherwise.

The shape is the same: the web, the raw CSV and the figure, and between them download, load, smooth and Trend.save, open here. The differences are the interesting part. A step gone or a place moved would be drift, and Atlas would show where. Here it is the language speaking:

The mathematics of your code.

Mathematics is a language that allows you to make things consistent. Best practices in coding are consistent, so it makes sense to view your computer code through a mathematical perspective. Because we can turn the mathematics of the code into a visual, you can literally see the consistency of your code, if you followed best practices. This is independent of the language you use: every program is drawn by the same mathematical rules.

What is inside

Category theory

A category of types
Types are objects, functions are arrows, call order is composition. A route is a composite from an entry point to a place.
Sum types are coproducts
Taken apart where they arise.
Lawful lenses
A class that writes a place and reads it back is a lens on that place. Atlas asks the put-get law at the level of types: what goes in as a Graph comes back as a Graph.

Topology

Zooming out glues, a filter is a fiber
Zooming out glues each family of types into one point: a quotient. Keeping to one route is the fiber over it, the part that lies above one point. Gluing and taking a fiber don't commute, so the order of a view is meaning.
Fine and coarse
A zoom level divides the nodes into boxes, and each such division is a topology on them. Zooming in and out moves between finer and coarser ones, so the picture stays the same picture.

Type theory

Effects in the type
A function's domain is its parameters plus what it reads; its codomain is what it returns plus what it writes, sends or runs, as in a type-and-effect system. Files, tables and URLs are objects like any other.
Subtypes keep their promise
An override does what its base says (behavioural subtyping, Liskov).

Graph theory

A step and its reach
A function's own arrow and everything it reaches are different quantities, never merged.
