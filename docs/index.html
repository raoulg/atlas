The same program. The same figure.

Fetch sunspot counts from the web, keep the CSV, clean it, smooth it, draw a figure. Written twice in Python: once as a sloppy script, once typed and elegant. Atlas draws what each one does.

the monthly mean sunspot number since 1749 (SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium), smoothed over 13 months · drawn by the typed pipeline

Both reach the same places: the web, the raw CSV, the figure. Inside they part. In the sloppy script every step reads and changes one global, Global(sunspots.df); nothing is handed from step to step. In the typed one a DataFrame goes from load to smooth to Trend.save, and the command names every file. A red dot is a question Atlas asks the author: why do get_data and plot pick their own files, and what do the four functions give back?

The mathematics of your code.

Atlas draws mathematics. Beneath it lies a small piece of category theory, and every construct has one shape: data at rest is a box of its kind, a function is an arrow, a route runs from a way in to the places it reaches. Nothing is guessed, and every program is drawn by the same rules. So you need none of the mathematics to read the picture: consistent code draws as a consistent picture, and where the picture looks off, so is the code.

What is inside, for the mathematically inclined
A category of types
Types are objects, functions are arrows, call order is composition. A route is a composite from an entry point to a place.
Effects in the type
A function's domain is its parameters plus what it reads; its codomain is what it returns plus what it writes, sends or runs, as in an effect system.
Zoom is a quotient, a filter is a fiber
Zooming out folds a family of types into one box; keeping to one route is the fiber over it. They don't commute, so the order of a view is meaning.
Sum types are coproducts
Taken apart where they arise.
Lawful lenses
A class that writes a place and reads it back is a lens on it. Atlas asks the put-get law at the level of types: what goes in as a Graph comes back as a Graph.
Subtypes keep their promise
An override does what its base says (Liskov).
A step and its reach
A function's own arrow and the composite it starts are different quantities, never merged.
